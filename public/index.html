What Zonorah is

Zonorah is a Media Neuroscience Lab. Our research links media and the brain. The Zonorah computational tool takes care of the media side: it listens to Peruvian radio, television and YouTube, transcribes what is said and measures how much, when, by whom and how each topic is framed.

The tool is software and does not record brain activity. Our research on the brain side is separate, and it draws on the tool's data about what was actually broadcast.

How it works

Step 01

Capture

We receive each station's live signal over the internet and record it in the time windows of its schedule.

Step 02

Transcribe

A speech-recognition model (Whisper) runs locally and transcribes the audio with a timestamp for every sentence. The audio is deleted as soon as it is transcribed.

Step 03

Analyse

Terms are searched in the transcript, ignoring case and accents and including variants. Each relevant passage is classified in closed fields: who is speaking, the role of the subject, topic, tone, framing, and whether a claim rests on verifiable evidence or on rumour.

Step 04

Measure

Public figures are minutes on air in which a term was said, per 100 hours recorded. Stations recorded for different lengths of time stay comparable.

Two fronts, one system

Scientific research

For journalists, teachers and researchers.

  • Published, literal rules that do not depend on any AI. The El Niño index, for example, does not count a bare “niño”, so the phenomenon is not confused with a minor.
  • Aggregate public figures, rounded to multiples of 5 and hidden below 3 minutes, so no one can tell whether a specific person was mentioned at a specific moment.
  • A comparator, embeddable charts, CSV downloads and a statistics laboratory.
  • An AI chat that answers from the transcripts and quotes short fragments with their station and time.

Commercial research

For organisations, agencies and brands.

  • You define the sources, keywords and rules. Zonorah listens and delivers a report on the schedule you choose.
  • Each mention is analysed: speaker, topic, tone, framing, evidence or rumour, reputational risk and urgency.
  • Reports go out as PDF by email, in English, Spanish or Indonesian.
  • Your commission is private: public data never reveals who asked for a follow-up or what was followed.
Commission research

Questions our data can answer

  • How much is a topic discussed on air, where and at what times?
  • Who has the voice: officials, companies, affected communities, experts, listeners?
  • How is the same event framed: as an environmental problem, an economic cost or a public-order issue?
  • Which claims rest on verifiable evidence, and which on opinion, rumour or denial?

Foundations

Zonorah takes the computational side of media neuroscience: measuring media messages at scale. The Media Neuroscience Lab at UC Santa Barbara, directed by René Weber, works, among other topics, on computational analysis of moral narratives and moral conflict in news and entertainment, text mining and large media datasets.

What the Zonorah tool does not do: brain imaging, experiments with participants, or any claim about what media do to a person's brain. It supplies the media-side data that research on the brain needs.

Limits you should know

Speech-recognition errors.
Transcription is automatic. A badly transcribed term is not counted, so figures tend to fall short, especially for proper names, acronyms and brands.
Coverage.
Only what was recorded is measured. “Not recorded” is not the same as “not said”.
Music and advertising.
Recorded minutes include songs and adverts. A term in an advert also counts.
Comparing stations.
A music station and a news station are not directly comparable. Compare stations of the same type, or the same term over time.
Rounding.
Figures are rounded, so adding groups may not give exactly the total. AI summaries come from automatic transcripts and can contain errors.

Questions

What is Zonorah?

Zonorah is a Media Neuroscience Lab. It listens to Peruvian radio, television and YouTube, transcribes what is said and measures how much each topic is discussed, where, when and by whom. It offers open data for scientific research and custom research for organisations.

What does Zonorah measure?

Minutes on air in which a term was said, per 100 hours recorded. Minutes, not mentions: a three-minute item that repeats a term ten times counts as three minutes.

Does Zonorah measure brain activity?

No. Zonorah is a computational tool and does not record brain activity. It measures the media side: what is broadcast, how much, when, by whom and how it is framed. Our research on the brain side is separate and draws on that data.

Does Zonorah keep recordings?

No. The audio is deleted as soon as it has been transcribed.

How accurate is it?

Transcription is automatic and makes errors, mostly with proper names, acronyms and brands, so figures tend to fall short. Each figure is published together with the hours recorded.
